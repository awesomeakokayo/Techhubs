## Stage 1 — Editing Fundamentals

**Objective:** Cut a clean timeline with correct audio and export settings.

**Success criteria:**
- Assemble a timeline
- Use cut types deliberately
- Export a correct file

### Instruction

Learn how editing software works and the fundamental language of cuts, transitions, and timing.

OBJECTIVE
Cut a clean timeline with correct audio and export settings.

SUCCESS CRITERIA
• Assemble a timeline
• Use cut types deliberately
• Export a correct file

WORKED EXAMPLE
Cut a 60-second clip from raw footage. Step 1: import and trim to the best take. Step 2: add a J or L cut so the audio leads or trails the picture smoothly. Step 3: normalise audio levels. Step 4: export at the platform's target resolution and bitrate. Watch it back on a phone before exporting again.

COMMON MISTAKES

• Exporting at the wrong resolution or bitrate

1. What is a J-cut?
   - A hard cut
   - **✔ Audio from the next clip starts before its picture**
   - A cut with no transition
   - A cut to black
   - _Answer:_ Audio from the next clip starts before its picture · _Why:_ In a J-cut the incoming audio leads the incoming video (the audio shape resembles a J). · _Level:_ understand

2. Why level audio before final export?
   - It changes the video resolution
   - **✔ So dialogue is consistently audible and nothing clips or is buried**
   - It reduces file size
   - It adds captions
   - _Answer:_ So dialogue is consistently audible and nothing clips or is buried · _Why:_ Consistent audio levels keep speech intelligible across the edit. · _Level:_ apply

3. A video looks soft after upload even though it looked sharp locally. What is the most likely cause?
   - The camera lens
   - **✔ Export settings (resolution/bitrate) too low for the platform**
   - Too much colour grading
   - The music choice
   - _Answer:_ Export settings (resolution/bitrate) too low for the platform · _Why:_ Under-spec export settings, or platform re-encoding, can soften a video. · _Level:_ analyze

## Stage 2 — Storytelling & Pacing

**Objective:** Structure an edit so viewers stay to the end.

**Success criteria:**
- Apply story structure
- Pace for the format
- Use music purposefully

### Instruction

Technical editing is the vehicle. Storytelling is the destination. Make people watch until the end.

OBJECTIVE
Structure an edit so viewers stay to the end.

SUCCESS CRITERIA
• Apply story structure
• Pace for the format
• Use music purposefully

WORKED EXAMPLE
Restructure a flat video. Step 1: open with the outcome rather than the setup. Step 2: add a hook in the first five seconds. Step 3: place a pattern interrupt (a cut, a question, a graphic) in the middle. Step 4: end on a clear call to action. Compare the retention curve before and after.

COMMON MISTAKES
• Slow openings that lose viewers
• Music that fights the dialogue

### Worked example

Restructure a flat video. Step 1: open with the outcome rather than the setup. Step 2: add a hook in the first five seconds. Step 3: place a pattern interrupt (a cut, a question, a graphic) in the middle. Step 4: end on a clear call to action. Compare the retention curve before and after.

### Common mistakes
- Slow openings that lose viewers
- Music that fights the dialogue

### Knowledge check (3 questions, server graded: yes)

1. What is the purpose of a strong opening in a short video?
   - To list credits
   - **✔ To earn the viewer's attention in the first seconds**
   - To show the title only
   - To increase the file size
   - _Answer:_ To earn the viewer's attention in the first seconds · _Why:_ Retention depends on giving viewers a reason to keep watching immediately. · _Level:_ apply

2. Pacing for a 60-second short compared with a 15-minute video should generally be…
   - Identical
   - **✔ Faster and denser, with quicker cuts**
   - Much slower
   - Unchanged by format
   - _Answer:_ Faster and denser, with quicker cuts · _Why:_ Short-form needs a higher information and cut rate to hold attention. · _Level:_ understand

3. When should music be adjusted?
   - Never
   - **✔ At story beats so it supports rather than fights the narrative**
   - Only at the end
   - To hide bad audio
   - _Answer:_ At story beats so it supports rather than fights the narrative · _Why:_ Aligning music to beats reinforces pacing and emotion. · _Level:_ apply

## Stage 4 — Platform-Specific Editing

**Objective:** Adapt edits to each platform's format and audience.

**Success criteria:**
- Edit for long-form
- Edit for Shorts/Reels
- Add captions

### Instruction

Different platforms have different editing languages. Master the nuances of each format.

OBJECTIVE
Adapt edits to each platform's format and audience.

SUCCESS CRITERIA
• Edit for long-form
• Edit for Shorts/Reels
• Add captions

WORKED EXAMPLE
Repurpose one edit for two platforms. Step 1: export a vertical 9:16 version with burned-in captions and a faster hook for short-form. Step 2: keep a horizontal 16:9 version for long-form. Step 3: adjust the pacing and the first-three-seconds for each. Step 4: note which version your audience completes more often.

COMMON MISTAKES
• Reusing a landscape edit unchanged for vertical
• Omitting captions on sound-off platforms

### Worked example
