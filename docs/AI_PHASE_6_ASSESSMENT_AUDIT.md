# Phase 6 — AI Assessment Audit

## Rule

Every AI verification question must answer:

> Where was this concept taught?

For the AI curriculum, the trace is explicit:

**Question → teaching stage → Learn → See → Practice → Verify**

A learner must never need to discover an unstated fact from the internet just to pass an AI stage.

## Assessment design standard

Every AI stage receives a focused multiple-choice assessment of 3–5 questions. When five questions are available, the intended cognitive progression is:

1. **Understand** — demonstrate comprehension of the taught principle.
2. **Apply** — choose how to use it in a realistic situation.
3. **Apply** — select an action that operationalizes the competency.
4. **Analyze** — diagnose evidence, tradeoffs, or failure modes.
5. **Evaluate** — make a professional judgment using the stage's success criteria.

This intentionally omits a mandatory Recall slot. Memorization is useful when necessary, but the AI curriculum prioritizes application and reasoning.

## Provenance implementation

`QuizQuestion` now supports:

- `teachingSource`
- `teachingStageId`
- `cognitiveLevel`

`lib/ai-assessment-audit.ts` populates these fields for every AI Verify question generated by the guided path.

The teaching source points back to the exact AI stage objective. The stage itself already contains the TechSkillHub Learn lesson, worked example, practice task, and mastery checkpoint. This makes the provenance inspectable in code rather than relying on a reviewer to infer it.

## Orphan-question policy

Questions are removed or rewritten when they depend on:

- facts that were never taught in the curriculum;
- tool-specific trivia that is not part of the competency;
- external reading that the learner was never told to study;
- memorization of a provider's branding or product details when those facts are irrelevant to the skill;
- a new technical concept that belongs to a later stage.

The fallback questions used by the AI path test the stage's stated success criteria, correction behavior, and evidence of applied mastery. They do not require outside facts.

## Quality gate

`validateAIAssessmentTrace()` rejects an AI assessment when:

- a question has no teaching-stage provenance;
- a question has no teaching source;
- a question has no cognitive-level classification;
- a five-question assessment has fewer than three Apply/Analyze/Evaluate questions;
- the assessment has fewer than three or more than five questions.

## Relationship to Phase 4

Phase 4 established the sequence:

**Learn → See → Practice → Verify → Build**

Phase 6 now makes the Verify step accountable to the earlier parts of that sequence. Verification is not allowed to test a concept that was not taught and practiced first.

## Relationship to Phase 5

Phase 5 audited projects backward. Phase 6 applies the same backward-trace principle to assessments:

**Assessment question → competency → taught stage → project/build application**

This keeps the curriculum internally coherent: learners are taught a skill, practice it, are assessed on it, and then use it in a build.

## Scope note

This phase deliberately audits the AI curriculum's verification layer. Existing non-AI track quizzes continue to use the legacy assessment model until those tracks receive a dedicated curriculum-and-assessment rebuild rather than being retroactively labeled as AI assessments.
