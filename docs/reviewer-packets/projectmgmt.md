## 5. Knowledge checks and answer keys (internal)

### Stage 1 — PM Foundations (3 questions)

1. What is the triple constraint?
   - · People, process, tools
   - **✔ Scope, time and cost trade-offs that define a project**
   - · Risk, quality, value
   - · Plan, do, review
   - _Why:_ Scope, time and cost are interdependent constraints; changing one affects the others. · _level: understand_
2. What is a work breakdown structure (WBS)?
   - · A Gantt chart
   - **✔ A hierarchical decomposition of the work into manageable pieces**
   - · A risk list
   - · A budget
   - _Why:_ A WBS decomposes deliverables into work packages. · _level: understand_
3. A stakeholder has high interest but low influence. How should you manage them?
   - · Ignore them
   - **✔ Keep them informed so their interest does not become disruption**
   - · Give them decision authority
   - · Escalate immediately
   - _Why:_ High-interest, low-influence stakeholders should be kept informed. · _level: apply_

### Stage 2 — Agile Principles (3 questions)

1. Which does the Agile Manifesto value more?
   - · Processes and tools over people
   - **✔ Responding to change over following a plan**
   - · Contracts over collaboration
   - · Documentation over working software
   - _Why:_ Agile values individuals, working software, collaboration and responding to change. · _level: understand_
2. When is a Waterfall approach more appropriate than Agile?
   - · When requirements are highly uncertain
   - **✔ When scope is stable, well understood and changes are costly**
   - · Always
   - · Never
   - _Why:_ Waterfall suits stable, well-understood scope; Agile suits uncertainty. · _level: evaluate_
3. "Agile means no planning" is a misconception because…
   - · Agile forbids plans
   - **✔ Agile plans continuously at multiple horizons rather than producing one upfront plan**
   - · Plans are illegal
   - · Agile has no backlog
   - _Why:_ Agile is continuous planning, not absence of planning. · _level: analyze_

### Stage 3 — Scrum in Practice (3 questions)

1. What is the Product Owner accountable for?
   - · Facilitating events
   - **✔ Maximising the value delivered by the product and ordering the backlog**
   - · Writing all code
   - · Managing the servers
   - _Why:_ The PO owns product value and the ordering of the backlog. · _level: understand_
2. What is the purpose of the sprint retrospective?
   - · Plan the next sprint
   - **✔ Inspect how the team worked and choose improvements**
   - · Demo to stakeholders
   - · Estimate stories
   - _Why:_ Retrospectives focus on process improvement. · _level: understand_
3. Why are story points relative rather than hours?
   - · They are faster
   - **✔ They express effort/complexity relative to other stories and are more stable across a team**
   - · They are required
   - · They are exact
   - _Why:_ Relative estimation is more reliable than false precision in hours. · _level: apply_

### Stage 4 — Tools & Planning (3 questions)

1. What is the critical path?
   - · The shortest task
   - **✔ The longest sequence of dependent tasks that determines the project duration**
   - · The highest-risk task
   - · The most expensive task
   - _Why:_ The critical path is the longest dependent chain; delay there delays the project. · _level: understand_
2. What is the purpose of a WIP limit on a Kanban board?
   - · To reduce quality
   - **✔ To limit work in progress so flow improves and bottlenecks surface**
   - · To hide work
   - · To estimate
   - _Why:_ Limiting WIP improves flow and reveals bottlenecks. · _level: apply_
3. What should you use to track an Agile sprint?
   - · A Gantt chart only
   - **✔ A backlog and board suited to the team's flow**
   - · A spreadsheet only
   - · Email threads
   - _Why:_ Agile tracking uses a backlog and board rather than a fixed Gantt only. · _level: understand_

### Stage 5 — Budgeting & Reporting (3 questions)

1. An SPI below 1.0 indicates…
   - · The project is ahead of schedule
   - **✔ The project is behind schedule**
   - · The project is under budget
   - · Nothing
   - _Why:_ SPI < 1 means schedule performance is behind plan. · _level: apply_
2. What is the main purpose of a status report?
   - · To list everything done
   - **✔ To communicate health, risks and decisions needed, concisely**
   - · To increase documentation
   - · To assign blame
   - _Why:_ Status reports communicate health and decisions, not activity logs. · _level: evaluate_
3. What is a lessons-learned exercise for?
   - · Punishing mistakes
   - **✔ Capturing what to repeat and change for future projects**
   - · Satisfying auditors only
   - · Filling time
   - _Why:_ Lessons learned improve future projects by capturing causes and improvements. · _level: understand_

### Stage 6 — Certifications & Career (3 questions)

1. Which certification is aimed at entry-level project managers?
   - · PMP
   - **✔ CAPM**
   - · PSM III
   - · CISSP
   - _Why:_ CAPM is the entry-level PMI credential; PMP requires experience. · _level: recall_
2. A PM portfolio is strongest when it shows…
   - · Certificates only
   - **✔ Real projects with charters, plans and measured outcomes**
   - · A long CV
   - · Tool lists
   - _Why:_ Evidence of real delivery is more persuasive than credentials alone. · _level: evaluate_
3. How can someone build PM experience without the title?
   - · Wait for a promotion
   - **✔ Run a side project, community initiative or university project with real planning and retrospectives**
   - · Take more courses only
   - · Avoid responsibility
   - _Why:_ Running a real project end-to-end creates genuine, documentable experience. · _level: apply_

### Stage 2 — Build: Agile vs Waterfall Decision Brief

- **Objective:** Compare Agile and predictive (waterfall) delivery and justify a choice.
- **Problem:** Teams often default to one delivery method without weighing the project's real conditions.
- **Audience:** A project sponsor deciding how to run a project.
- **Required functionality:**
  - Two contrasting project scenarios
  - A comparison across uncertainty, scope stability, regulation, and stakeholder access
  - A justified recommendation per scenario
- **Acceptance criteria:**
  - Each recommendation follows from the stated conditions
  - Trade-offs and risks of the chosen method are acknowledged
  - Both scenarios name the specific factors (uncertainty, scope stability, regulation, stakeholder access) that drove the choice
- **Required evidence:**
  - A decision brief for both scenarios
  - A comparison matrix
  - A short risk note per choice
- **Constraints:**
  - Base the comparison on the project conditions, not preference
  - Cite the factors explicitly
